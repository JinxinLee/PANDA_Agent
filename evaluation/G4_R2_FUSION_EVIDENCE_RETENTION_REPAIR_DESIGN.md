## Current R2 boundary and cause

`Retriever._collect_channel_rankings` collects bounded `exact`, `dense`, `sparse`, conditional `paper`, `workflow`, and `graph` streams. The policy's `candidate_pool_per_channel` is 20 and `final_evidence_limit` is 12. The current `RetrievalPlan` carries `source_budgets`, `required_source_types`, target repositories, resolved versions, and paper hints from the selected intent policy and question-grounded obligations. `algorithm_theory`, for example, gives paper a 0.55 budget while its required-source list may be empty. The dedicated `_paper` channel currently runs only when paper is required, but paper objects may still arrive through other channels. In the production single-pass `retrieve` path, each channel contributes `weight / (60 + one_based_rank)`; insertion-stable score sorting is truncated to 30 before `_apply_structured_replacement` may reserve up to three local bridge candidates. `collect_channel_candidates` instead captures those channel streams and eligible structured supplements without a local LLM rerank.

The global `consolidate_and_select_candidates` path merges initial, pre-answer targeted, and E3 targeted pass snapshots by `object_id`. It retains `payloads`, `pass_occurrences`, and each object's minimum one-based rank per channel; repeated passes do not add score. Conflicting source/version/locator/text/type/title payloads return `consistency_failure`. Global weighted RRF uses `exact=2.0`, `dense=1.0`, `sparse=1.0`, `paper=1.15`, `workflow=1.2`, `graph=0.8`, with `rrf_k=60`; ties in `base_fused_order` break by lexicographic `object_id`. Ordered `eligible_supplemental_ids` receive no fabricated channel rank. Their first 30 unique IDs take slots first, then ordinary RRF fills `30 - len(supp_pool)` slots. The actual pool order is ordinary base candidates followed by supplements. One global LLM call sees only this pool and the original question. Later ordering includes reranked IDs, the pool, and fused IDs, followed by the unchanged final selector and its source/type/locator budgets. Thus an out-of-pool object can still occur in a later fallback ordering; the diagnosed loss is specifically its missing **semantic rerank opportunity**, not proof that final selection is impossible under every path.

Single-channel evidence contributes only one term to RRF. Several moderately ranked, multi-channel objects can accumulate more terms and take the 30 slots despite being less useful for a particular question. The global model cannot compare a truncated object with those survivors. This is an R2 pre-rerank retention defect, even when upstream recall and downstream conservative verification behave as intended. It does not establish an R1, R3, or V1 repair mandate.

## Options considered

| Option | Benefit | Cost and reason not selected |
|---|---|---|
| A. Larger global pool | More omissions may reach the model. | More candidate text/tokens and comparison load on every affected call; another finite cutoff remains, and rerank quality could deteriorate. |
| B. Equal per-channel quotas | Fixed model budget and simple channel coverage. | Channel identity alone misses question-specific source roles; active fallback streams could displace consensus. |
| C. Change fusion score | Could raise single-channel ranks without a second pool path. | A global score change perturbs every ordering, weakens interpretability of existing weights/consensus, and needs empirical calibration. |
| D. Bounded policy-role frontier plus current RRF | Gives valid, omitted candidates a bounded opportunity in roles already allocated by the query plan while the strict majority of non-supplement slots remains RRF-selected. | A limited frontier cannot guarantee every relevant item survives; acceptance must check precision and the exposed symptom. **Selected.** |
| E. Obligation-aware retention | Might target evidence for a missing answer point. | At this R2 seam the original question and plan exist, but later G1 answer-point/verification judgments are not authoritative inputs. Pulling them into fusion adds model dependence and circular ownership; no such change is justified by the diagnosed truncation. |

## Selected pool contract for a future implementation

Operate once over consolidated `best_ranks`, `pass_occurrences`, and consistent `payloads`, after ordinary scores and ordered structured supplements are known. Apply the same pool constructor to a single captured pass and a multi-pass consolidation where appropriate; no E3-specific retention route. Do not change RRF weights, `rrf_k`, the configured channel limit, the global model input shape, or final selection. The only upstream compatibility addition needed for specialized-channel eligibility is a minimal internal normal-versus-generic-fallback origin marker at the existing `_workflow` and `_graph` branch returns; it does not change which objects those channels retrieve.

1. Form `supp_pool` exactly as today: the first 30 unique eligible structured-supplement IDs in existing order. Its existing reservation has precedence over the frontier. Let `base_slots = 30 - len(supp_pool)`. Form the ordinary base RRF order excluding `supp_pool`; its first `base_slots` IDs are the unchanged counterfactual base pool.
2. Define an **evidence role**, not a channel quota, from each source type with positive `plan.source_budgets[role]` or membership in `plan.required_source_types`. A role absent from both has no frontier right. For a budgeted role, its maximum number of challenger opportunities is `max(1, ceil(final_evidence_limit * plan.source_budgets[role]))`, matching the existing final selector's source-type budget calculation; a required role without a positive budget gets one minimum opportunity. These are ceilings, never reserved slots. They permit complementary chunks from one role when the existing plan allocates multiple final-evidence positions, without using answer points or exposed-case ranks. For `graph`, the plan's graph budget supplies an opportunity ceiling even though `_source_type_of` does not return `graph`; this is **not** a claim that the current final selector enforces a graph type cap.
3. Assign role membership from the existing candidate payload and legitimate channel occurrence. `_source_type_of(payload)` supplies code, paper, documentation, readme, or workflow source roles regardless of whether the object came through `exact`, `dense`, or `sparse`; these are retrieval mechanisms, not source roles. In addition, a valid `workflow` or `graph` channel occurrence may support its corresponding plan role, including when `_source_type_of` classifies the underlying object as code. Thus a paper object returned by dense/sparse is eligible under a positive paper budget even if the dedicated paper channel was not run; a query-grounded workflow or accepted-relation graph object is eligible when its plan role is positive or required. No intent-name branch or static channel whitelist is introduced. An object may belong to more than one role but can take only one rerank slot.
4. A challenger must be outside `supp_pool` and the unchanged counterfactual base pool, pass the existing source/version/payload validity gates, and have at least one qualifying occurrence. `exact`, `dense`, `sparse`, and the conditional `paper` channel are normal occurrences. `_workflow` currently returns either question-matched SQL results or a curated fallback; `_graph` returns either accepted-relation traversal results or a curated structural fallback. Their current payloads and pass snapshots do **not** reliably distinguish these branches for a curated object. A future implementation must carry only an internal per-occurrence `normal` versus `generic_fallback` bit from these two existing branches: a sidecar aligned with each channel's ranked items in a pass snapshot is sufficient, and consolidation can attach the bit to its existing `pass_occurrences`. It must not alter canonical source payload identity or public traces. A generic-fallback occurrence gives no frontier preference, while a normal specialized occurrence may. If that bit is absent in a historical snapshot, the specialized occurrence is ineligible for frontier preference rather than guessed from source ID, object type, or rank. The same object may still qualify through an independently valid normal occurrence in another channel and always remains eligible for ordinary RRF. This is a narrow branch-origin fact, not a new provenance or version authority system.
5. For each active role, order its eligible omitted candidates by `(best qualifying channel rank, -existing RRF score, object_id)`. Let `role_slots` be the lesser of its policy-derived ceiling and its count of eligible omitted candidates. Let `frontier_capacity = min(sum(role_slots), max(0, floor((base_slots - 1) / 2)))`. The second term is exactly the largest frontier that leaves a **strict majority** of non-supplement slots for RRF, as required by this design's consensus contract; it is not a chosen percentage. Visit roles in deterministic order: required roles first, then decreasing positive source budget, then role name. In successive rounds, offer the next not-yet-selected candidate from each role until `frontier_capacity` is reached or all role queues are exhausted. Duplicate objects are skipped and the role's next candidate is considered. An unused role opportunity consumes no slot and returns to RRF. A low-ranked object has no unconditional protection merely because it is single-channel: it competes behind higher-ranked eligible omitted objects in its role and all rights remain capacity-bound.
6. Take the first `base_slots - len(frontier_selected)` ordinary RRF IDs as the backbone, skipping selected frontier IDs; fill any unused slots in normal RRF order. Offer `[RRF backbone, frontier-selected, supp_pool]` to the unchanged reranker. Keep `base_fused_order`, scores, and full channel/pass provenance unchanged. The pool is unique by canonical `object_id` and never exceeds 30. With 30 supplements, `base_slots` and frontier capacity are zero. For every other capacity, ordinary RRF retains a strict majority of the non-supplement positions.

The opportunity ceiling comes from existing per-query source-role allocation and the existing final-evidence capacity, while the global cap follows from the fixed rerank pool and the explicit RRF-majority requirement. Multiple candidates from one role can be protected only to the extent that the plan already allocates room for that role; one per channel would be insufficient for complementary chunks. The contract does not promise all high-ranked single-channel objects survive. Synthetic controls and exposed non-gating replay must report any remaining loss honestly, including supplemental saturation.

```text
FRONTIER_CAPACITY_DERIVATION = min(sum(min(policy_role_capacity, valid_omitted_candidates_per_role)), strict_RRF_majority_residual)
ELIGIBILITY_DERIVATION = positive plan source budget or required source role, plus matching valid payload and normal channel occurrence
ELIGIBILITY_ABSTRACTION = hybrid source-role / channel occurrence / provenance
FALLBACK_ELIGIBILITY = generic fallback occurrence has no frontier preference; ordinary RRF remains available
STRUCTURED_SUPPLEMENT_PRECEDENCE = existing supp_pool reservation first, frontier only within base_slots
RRF_BACKBONE_GUARANTEE = more than half of non-supplement slots stay in ordinary RRF order
GLOBAL_RERANK_BOUND = 30 / unchanged
```

### Validity and authority boundaries

The existing retrieval channels constrain target repository and resolved source version: exact SQL, vector query filters, paper source/version filters, and workflow/graph SQL use the plan's allowed sources and versions. Structured eligibility stays in `_apply_structured_replacement`. Before granting a *new frontier preference*, require the consolidated payload's canonical `object_id` and fields needed by the current reranker and selector, and reuse the same plan target/source and resolved-version authority used by channel filters. Reject an inconsistent payload or an unresolvable targeted version; do not create a separate source/version inference path. Context-source eligibility follows the existing channel rules; high rank alone never grants validity. Existing `consistency_failure` remains prior to pool construction. Invalid/wrong-version objects may not gain frontier protection. Existing final locator, source-budget, admission, and citation checks still apply to all candidates.

## Deterministic controls to instantiate before implementation

Use fabricated objects and plans only. Build the current and proposed pool from the same frozen synthetic snapshots; use a fake reranker only where final-selector compatibility needs checking. Do not derive inputs or constants from the exposed cases.

| Control | Synthetic contract | Expected before repair |

R2-A is the required generic product RED. Its variants should cover complementary chunks from one role within the policy-derived role capacity, and a single active role without claiming universal recall. R2-I is the explicit non-code policy control; R2-F/R2-I pair normal and fallback provenance. R2-B/C/F/G/H are opposing safety controls: a repair fails if it keeps R2-A only by admitting arbitrary noise, invalid evidence, or extra cost. Existing G4 18/18 dual-sided baseline and focused G1/G2/G3/E3 retrieval neighbors remain required after implementation; no tests are executed by this design.

## Observability and replay

Current consolidation exposes `best_channel_ranks`, `pass_occurrences`, `scores`, `fused_candidate_ids`, and `reranked_object_ids`; saved retrieval traces expose ordered channel/fused candidates and model-returned reranked candidates. A model may return only a subset of offered IDs. Neither `reranked_object_ids` nor the fused list proves the exact offered pool or whether an ID entered through supplement, frontier, or RRF. `ADDITIONAL_TRACE_FIELD_JUSTIFIED = true`: `rerank_pool_entries` is an ordered, additive **diagnostic-only** list of `{object_id, reason}` with `reason` in `structured_supplemental | policy_role_frontier | ordinary_rrf`. It supports acceptance diagnostics, causal verification, and offline comparison; it is not behavior authority or a prerequisite for computing the pool. Keep it in existing internal retrieval-stage diagnostics, with no public DTO, answer semantics, or formal trace-schema change. For overlaps, reason precedence is supplemental, then frontier, then RRF; the single slot retains all existing rank/provenance records. The separate internal specialized-channel origin bit supplies behavior eligibility and need not create a public or formal trace field.

After generic RED→GREEN, an offline deterministic pool replay may use already frozen, compatible G3 channel rankings, payloads, and eligible supplements for exposed `n002`, `n006`, and `n017`. It asks only whether the previously lost objects enter the **offered rerank pool** under the predeclared rule. These three cases cannot select role capacities or establish independent generalization; report any miss honestly. Historical workflow/graph occurrences without the origin bit cannot be classified as normal for frontier purposes; do not fabricate their provenance. `fusion_replay.py` is evaluation-only and models weighted fusion over frozen channels, but its historical C6 `CURRENT` policy does not model global multi-pass structured-supplement reservation. A future replay must faithfully reproduce the current global constructor and the proposed change; do not edit C6 historical policy semantics or infer order from a serialized score-map key order.

Saved LLM rerank output cannot rank newly admitted objects it never saw. Pool-retention replay is therefore separate from post-rerank selection, answer quality, and false-insufficiency outcomes. Those questions may later require separately authorized live model execution; none is authorized or claimed here.
