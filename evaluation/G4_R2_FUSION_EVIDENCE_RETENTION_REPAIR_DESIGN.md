# G4 R2 Fusion Evidence-Retention Repair Design

## Decision and authority

`SELECTED_DESIGN = BOUNDED_CORE_CHANNEL_FRONTIER_WITH_RRF_BACKBONE`.

This is a design-only decision for `G4-R2-CHANNEL-EVIDENCE-LOSS`, established on the exposed G4 Lane A diagnostic. It does not authorize an implementation, a replay, dataset curation, retrieval, QA, or model execution. The three attributed cases belong to independent information-need families, but the outcome-conditioned six-case subset supplies neither prevalence nor fresh confirmation. The one-family Q1 candidate, the two unresolved cases, and the separate Gold/source discrepancy do not define this repair. G3 admission `NO_CHANGE` and G1/G2/G3/G4 safety contracts remain in force.

The repair grants **rerank opportunity only**. It never grants final evidence, citation, claim, answer, or verification authority. The global reranker, `_prioritize_and_select_evidence`, `select_final_evidence`, `final_evidence_limit`, admission, and completeness verification retain their current roles. `RERANK_SYSTEM_PROMPT` is unchanged.

## Current R2 boundary and cause

`Retriever._collect_channel_rankings` collects bounded `exact`, `dense`, `sparse`, conditional `paper`, `workflow`, and `graph` streams. The policy's `candidate_pool_per_channel` is 20. In the production single-pass `retrieve` path, each channel contributes `weight / (60 + one_based_rank)`; insertion-stable score sorting is truncated to 30 before `_apply_structured_replacement` may reserve up to three local bridge candidates. `collect_channel_candidates` instead captures those channel streams and eligible structured supplements without a local LLM rerank.

The global `consolidate_and_select_candidates` path merges initial, pre-answer targeted, and E3 targeted pass snapshots by `object_id`. It retains `payloads`, `pass_occurrences`, and each object's minimum one-based rank per channel; repeated passes do not add score. Conflicting source/version/locator/text/type/title payloads return `consistency_failure`. Global weighted RRF uses `exact=2.0`, `dense=1.0`, `sparse=1.0`, `paper=1.15`, `workflow=1.2`, `graph=0.8`, with `rrf_k=60`; ties in `base_fused_order` break by lexicographic `object_id`. Ordered `eligible_supplemental_ids` receive no fabricated channel rank. Their first 30 unique IDs take slots first, then ordinary RRF fills `30 - len(supp_pool)` slots. The actual pool order is ordinary base candidates followed by supplements. One global LLM call sees only this pool and the original question. Later ordering includes reranked IDs, the pool, and fused IDs, followed by the unchanged final selector and its source/type/locator budgets. Thus an out-of-pool object can still occur in a later fallback ordering; the diagnosed loss is specifically its missing **semantic rerank opportunity**, not proof that final selection is impossible under every path.

Single-channel evidence contributes only one term to RRF. Several moderately ranked, multi-channel objects can accumulate more terms and take the 30 slots despite being less useful for a particular question. The global model cannot compare a truncated object with those survivors. This is an R2 pre-rerank retention defect, even when upstream recall and downstream conservative verification behave as intended. It does not establish an R1, R3, or V1 repair mandate.

## Options considered

| Option | Benefit | Cost and reason not selected |
|---|---|---|
| A. Larger global pool | More omissions may reach the model. | More candidate text/tokens and comparison load on every affected call; another finite cutoff remains, and rerank quality could deteriorate. |
| B. Equal per-channel quotas | Fixed model budget and simple channel coverage. | Treats graph/workflow as peers of direct retrieval; active weak streams can displace consensus even when no useful single-channel item exists. |
| C. Change fusion score | Could raise single-channel ranks without a second pool path. | A global score change perturbs every ordering, weakens interpretability of existing weights/consensus, and needs empirical calibration. |
| D. Bounded core-channel frontier plus current RRF | Uses a small, conditional part of the existing budget for the best *omitted* direct-channel candidates while most slots remain RRF-selected. | A limited frontier cannot guarantee every relevant item survives; acceptance must check precision and the exposed symptom. **Selected.** |
| E. Obligation-aware retention | Might target evidence for a missing answer point. | At this R2 seam the original question and plan exist, but later G1 answer-point/verification judgments are not authoritative inputs. Pulling them into fusion adds model dependence and circular ownership; no such change is justified by the diagnosed truncation. |

## Selected pool contract for a future implementation

Operate once over consolidated `best_ranks`, `pass_occurrences`, and consistent `payloads`, after ordinary scores and ordered structured supplements are known. Apply the same pool constructor to a single captured pass and a multi-pass consolidation where appropriate; no E3-specific retention route. Do not change channel collection, RRF weights, `rrf_k`, the configured channel limit, the global model input shape, or final selection.

1. Form `supp_pool` exactly as today: first 30 unique eligible structured-supplement IDs in their existing order. These remain the highest-priority reservation because the structured replacement contract already governs their eligibility and was designed to protect a separate retrieval contribution. Let `base_slots = 30 - len(supp_pool)`.
2. Form the ordinary base RRF list excluding `supp_pool`. The unchanged counterfactual base pool is its first `base_slots` IDs. Let `frontier_budget = floor(base_slots / 5)` (at most six because the global limit is 30). This reserves at most one fifth of **non-supplemental** capacity; at least four fifths stays with ordinary RRF. With fewer than five base slots, no frontier reservation is made. The fraction is a predeclared cost/precision guard tied to the fixed pool, not to exposed channel ranks.
3. Eligible frontier channels are `exact`, `dense`, and `sparse`, plus `paper` only when the plan requires paper evidence. These are direct evidence streams with explicit query/source semantics. `exact` retains its existing high RRF weight and is eligible only when it actually has an omitted candidate; it receives no fixed quota. The dedicated paper channel already diversifies paper recall upstream, so it joins only for a paper-required plan. `workflow` and `graph` retain their normal weighted RRF access and existing downstream source-type handling, but get no independent reservation: graph expansion and workflow fallback are weaker grounds for displacing direct or consensus candidates. No channel's mere activation consumes a slot.
4. A frontier challenger must already be in a valid, usable channel stream and outside the unchanged ordinary base pool and `supp_pool`. It is never synthesized from Gold, an expected answer, an arbitrary path, or a known case. For each challenger, take the best `(rank, -channel_weight, channel_name)` among its eligible channels as its claiming channel. Sort challengers by `(best_rank, -claiming_channel_weight, channel_name, -rrf_score, object_id)`. Select up to `frontier_budget`, with at most `ceil(frontier_budget / 2)` claimed by one channel when at least two eligible channels have valid omitted challengers. If only one has challengers, allow it the entire frontier budget. This prevents a newly active weak or broad stream from mechanically taking the whole diversification budget; unused frontier slots revert to RRF. Both global and per-channel caps are architectural shares of the existing 30 slots, not ranks inferred from Lane A.
5. Use the first `base_slots - len(frontier_selected)` ordinary RRF IDs as the backbone, skipping any selected frontier IDs. Fill any unused places in normal RRF order. Pass `[RRF backbone, frontier-selected, supp_pool]` to the existing reranker. Keep `base_fused_order`, scores, and channel provenance unchanged. The pool is unique by canonical `object_id` and never exceeds 30. A multi-channel object can occupy only one slot, while its full best-rank and pass-occurrence record remains available.

The frontier gives the highest-ranked omitted direct-channel evidence a chance to be judged semantically, without reserving a fixed number for every active channel or promising that all high-ranked single-channel objects survive. As supplemental reservations grow, fewer frontier positions exist; with 30 supplements there are none. This explicitly preserves the current structured replacement priority. The future synthetic and exposed replay gates must report when supplemental saturation prevents retention rather than silently declaring the repair effective.

### Validity and authority boundaries

The existing retrieval channels constrain target repository and resolved source version: exact SQL, vector query filters, paper source/version filters, and workflow/graph SQL use the plan's allowed sources and versions. Structured eligibility stays in `_apply_structured_replacement`. Before granting a *new frontier preference*, recheck that the consolidated payload has canonical `object_id`, the fields needed by the current reranker and selector, allowed source provenance, and the plan-resolved version for a targeted repository; fail closed for an inconsistent payload or unresolvable targeted version. This is a preference eligibility check, not permission to widen a channel or replace its authoritative query filter. Context-source eligibility follows the existing channel rules; it is not inferred from a high rank alone. Existing `consistency_failure` remains prior to pool construction. Invalid/wrong-version objects may not gain frontier protection. Existing final locator, source-budget, admission, and citation checks still apply to all candidates.

## Observability and replay

Current consolidation exposes `best_channel_ranks`, `pass_occurrences`, `scores`, `fused_candidate_ids`, and `reranked_object_ids`; saved retrieval traces expose ordered channel/fused candidates and model-returned reranked candidates. A model may return only a subset of offered IDs. Neither `reranked_object_ids` nor the fused list proves the exact offered pool or whether an ID entered through supplement, frontier, or RRF. Thus **one additive diagnostic-only field is justified**: `rerank_pool_entries`, an ordered list of `{object_id, reason}` with `reason` in `structured_supplemental | channel_frontier | ordinary_rrf`. It belongs in the existing internal retrieval-stage diagnostics, with no public DTO, answer semantics, or formal trace-schema change. For overlaps, reason precedence is supplemental, then frontier, then RRF; the single slot retains all existing rank/provenance records. Future offline replay can compute the same field from frozen inputs without changing historical traces.

After generic RED→GREEN, an offline deterministic pool replay may use already frozen, compatible G3 channel rankings, payloads, and eligible supplements for exposed `n002`, `n006`, and `n017`. It asks only whether the previously lost objects enter the **offered rerank pool** under the predeclared rule. These three cases cannot select constants or establish independent generalization; report any miss honestly. `fusion_replay.py` is evaluation-only and models weighted fusion over frozen channels, but its historical C6 `CURRENT` policy does not model global multi-pass structured-supplement reservation. A future replay must faithfully reproduce the current global constructor and the proposed change; do not edit C6 historical policy semantics or infer order from a serialized score-map key order.

Saved LLM rerank output cannot rank newly admitted objects it never saw. Pool-retention replay is therefore separate from post-rerank selection, answer quality, and false-insufficiency outcomes. Those questions may later require separately authorized live model execution; none is authorized or claimed here.
